Case study 01

Upstox Native Account Opening (NAO) 2.0

Redesigning how people open an account at Upstox: a systems-level UX redesign and onboarding architecture overhaul, on one of India’s largest retail broking platforms, with 10M+ registered users and about 3M active traders.

Role
Product designer, project owner
Timeline
August 2022 – February 2023
Sector
Fintech, retail broking
Target metric
Lead → Account Created, 21.47% → 60.0%

at a glance

Upstox is India’s Robinhood equivalent, and every new investor starts by opening an account in the app. That flow was losing most of its leads: everything was typed by hand, and nothing was checked until a scrutiny team reviewed the application afterwards. I rebuilt it as blocks the team could reorder from a dashboard, and redesigned six of the steps where people dropped off or were rejected, so each one checks the input while the person is still on it.

  • 21.47%of leads became accounts before NAO 2.0; 35% of applications were rejected.
  • +4.9ptsemail step conversion with Google / Apple sign-in, 85.0% → 89.9%.
  • +3.56ptsPAN step conversion with an Equifax background check, 71.73% → 75.29%.
  • 9mandatory manual fields, against 12–15 at Zerodha, Angel One and Groww.

1. Executive summary & overall business objective

The challenge

Opening a broking account in India means a Demat account, KYC and SEBI registration, and like most things with government paperwork behind them, it’s slow. Upstox’s old flow had users type their email, PAN, bank details and signature by hand, and checked none of it until a scrutiny team reviewed the application afterwards.

Only 21.47% of leads ended up with an account. 35% of submitted applications were rejected, and 40% of the accounts that did get opened needed a sales call to get there.

Where things stood:

  • Classic flow conversion (organic): 12.94%
  • NAO 1.0 conversion (organic): 22.72%
  • Overall funnel conversion, all channels: 21.47% (NAO alone: 31.63%)
  • Rejection rate: 35%
  • Drop-off across the funnel: ~60%

The objective

The goal was to redesign and engineer NAO 2.0 to:

  1. Nearly double overall conversion to 60.0%.
  2. Reduce overall step-level rejection rates from 35.0% to 10.0% by replacing post-submission manual audit checks with real-time, automated verification APIs.
  3. Reduce median Time-to-Account Creation from 30 minutes to under 20 minutes, with a downstream system capability to open accounts instantly (under 2 hours).
  4. Decrease customer support overhead (historically, 40.0% of all completed accounts required active sales assistance calls to navigate the funnel).

3. Experience philosophy: the “N-star” & screen format frameworks

We moved away from isolated, superficial UI updates, to framing our redesign around two strategic systems:

A. The N-star ladder

Airbnb’s 11-star exercise, adapted so design, engineering and ops could agree on what “better” meant, from 1 (broken) to 10 (someone does it all for you):

  • 1-Star: User cannot complete signup due to platform bugs.
  • 4-Star: User opens the account in a single session without human assistance.
  • 5-Star: Instant activation. As soon as the user e-signs, they can trade immediately.
  • 6-Star: Zero manual input. Entering a mobile number pulls investment history and net worth in the background with user permission.
  • 10-Star: Founder-level onboarding. Fully personalized experience, direct welcoming calls from executives, premium physical welcome merchandise delivered to the user’s home.

What changed from 1 star to 10

The point of the ladder was to name what changes between each step, because that difference is what the design has to deliver. Read upwards, every step takes one more job off the user:

  • 1 to 4: they stop needing help. First the bugs go, then the sales calls and shared OTPs, until one person can finish in a single sitting.
  • 4 to 5: they stop waiting. The account works the moment they e-sign.
  • 5 to 6: they stop typing. Details and existing investments are fetched from the mobile number.
  • 6 to 9: they stop doing the setup. Bank linked in the background, salary invested automatically, existing holdings moved in one click.
  • 9 to 10: they stop doing it at all. Someone opens the account for them, and what is left is being looked after: calls from the founders, pro customers and support, and a welcome kit at home.

NAO 2.0 was aimed at making 4 and 5 stars the default, with the fetched data (SSO email, Equifax PAN, UPI bank details) as the first steps toward 6.

B. The screen format framework

Classified onboarding interfaces into three functional buckets to standardize the UI patterns and cognitive load across all screens:

  1. Simplified format: Applied to low cognitive-load steps. Focuses on minimizing actions (easy steps = fewest possible taps). Example: Email verification via one-tap Google/Apple SSO.
  2. Hand-holding format: Applied to high-friction, error-prone steps requiring external lookups. Features in-context instructional overlays, GIFs, or videos. Example: Real-time interactive canvas for digital signatures with explicit boundary-rule cues.
  3. Education format: Applied when we need the user to retain critical regulatory information or build trust in the platform’s security. Example: Front-loaded platform credibility carousel highlighting Upstox’s 1Cr+ investor base.

4. Templates and blocks

The old flow was a fixed, hard-coded sequence of steps. NAO 2.0 moved it to the backend-driven Templates and Blocks Architecture, so the funnel could be changed and experimented on without an app release.

Key technical specs

  • Blocks: Self-contained functional units (e.g. Email Verification Block, Liveness Block, Bank Verification Block) responsible for executing specific inputs, API integrations, and rule processing.
  • Templates: Compiled sequences of blocks.
  • Operator dashboard (CMS): Enables PMs and Engineering Managers to drag, drop, and configure onboarding flows dynamically without code releases.
    • UX toggles: Toggling explanatory video overlays on/off per step.
    • System thresholds: Remotely updating backend rule variables (e.g. changing the fuzzy name match pass score from 80 to 90 via CMS).
    • Dynamic template assignment: Segmenting users dynamically and serving custom onboarding flows (Templates) based on user demographic or intent.

5. The blocks, redesigned

Each block below starts with what went wrong for the person, then what changed.

AsignupBemailCbankDselfieEsignatureFe-sign

Block A: Signup, splash, and carousel (the entry point)

The friction point: High latency in cold-app loading, static landing layouts, and manual typing of phone numbers on Android devices.

The redesign solution:

  1. Optimized asset pipeline to establish minimal splash screen time. If start-up ran long, an animated loader appeared at the bottom so the app never looked stuck; if the network dropped, a notice took its place.
  1. Developed a remote-configured, auto-advancing Dynamic Media Carousel Space supporting images, GIFs, and videos. Carousel adjusts dynamically to 3-second intervals for photos, or matches video/GIF playback durations, with auto-adjustment patterns when the virtual keyboard opens.
  1. Integrated the Android Hint Module API to auto-suggest active phone numbers stored on the device, allowing one-tap selection and automating OTP reading and autofill.

Block B: SSO email verification

The friction point: Manual input of email addresses up to 60 characters long resulted in a 7.0% drop-off rate, primarily due to typographic errors, fake emails, or manual OTP entry friction.

The redesign solution:

  1. Implemented native Google Sign-In and Apple Sign-In (exclusive to iOS), converting email verification into a single-tap action.
  1. Engineered a backend verified duplicate-checking routine that validates inputs against existing lead databases and active verified accounts in real time to prevent duplicate signups.
  1. Created a real-time auto-verification mechanism for SSO emails. These accounts bypass manual scrutiny check-queues entirely, dropping off the scrutiny officer’s dashboard to save operational bandwidth.

Block C: Bank account verification

The friction point: Manually typing account numbers, re-entering them, searching for IFSC codes, and uploading cancelled cheques resulted in a 4.53% step-level drop-off and 15.53% of all rejections, due to typo mismatch.

The redesign solution:

  1. Introduced a Unified Payments Interface (UPI) reverse penny drop (via Setu): The user initiates a nominal 1 Rupee transaction via whitelisted local UPI apps. Setu captures the account holder’s name, account number, and IFSC directly from the UPI credit-alert webhook, then automatically refunds the 1 Rupee back to the user.
  2. Forward penny drop automation (backup path): For manual entries, the system deposits 1 Rupee and triggers real-time checking via Digio API (processing transactions in 4 seconds compared to Razorpay’s 16 seconds).
  3. Implemented a Fuzzy name match algorithm: Matches the fetched bank name with the Income Tax (PAN) record name. A matched score of ≥ 70 allows the user to proceed automatically. Any score below 70 is flagged for manual scrutiny.

Block D: Digital identity & liveness verification

The friction point: Static live photo submission checks took place post-form submission, introducing high processing delays. Poor camera angles or lighting led to 18.45% of all rejections.

The redesign solution:

  1. Integrated HyperVerge Liveness SDK directly into the front-end capture flow, shifting photo verification to a real-time step.
  1. Implemented front-end photo capture guidelines (lighting cues, head centering circle overlay) and dynamic edge-assessment to verify image clarity prior to capture.
  1. Backend performs real-time liveness threshold check (currently configured at an AI confidence score of > 75).
  1. Facial matching confidence score: For non-KRA users, the system compares the live capture against official government records in real time, automatically routing failed matches or low-confidence scores to the scrutiny desk.

Block E: Digital signature canvas

The friction point: Signature rejections accounted for 21.32% of total onboarding rejections, primarily due to signature cropping, illegible scribbles, or users drawing signatures inside a portrait container with restricted width.

The redesign solution:

  1. Designed a GIF/Media-driven priming module showing animated, language-agnostic instructions on how to sign within the boundaries.
  1. Constructed a landscape-oriented digital signature canvas that automatically opens in landscape mode upon interaction, maximizing canvas area and preventing stroke-clipping.
  2. A faint example signature to follow inside the canvas.
  1. Two guards back it up: a minimum-stroke check before submitting, and a blank canvas after a rejection, so the same failed signature can’t be sent twice.

Block F: E-sign custom UI integration

The friction point: The standard NSDL/UIDAI interface was complex, cluttered, and highly drop-off prone (~10.0% overall drop-off on E-Sign), heavily damaging mobile user trust.

The redesign solution:

  1. Collaborated with Setu to replace the standard portal with a customizable mobile-first UI.
  2. Repackaged and surfaced key information clearly to minimize user concern over entering sensitive Aadhaar numbers on non-Upstox interfaces.
  3. Expected step drop-off reduction of ~50.0%.

7. Business impact & metric validation

The rollout of individual modules within the NAO 2.0 initiative delivered immediate, quantifiable improvements across our organic cohorts:

Measured cohort performance

  1. PAN verification block (Equifax): Shifting to automated Equifax background verification raised step-level conversion from 71.73% to 75.29% (+3.56 percentage points).
  2. Email verification block (SSO): Incorporating Google/Apple SSO elevated conversion from 85.0% to 89.9% (+4.90 percentage points).
  3. Liveness verification block (HyperVerge): Transitioning to real-time image analysis predicted an overall conversion lift of +2.19% by reducing photo drop-offs to 1.0%.
  4. Overall NAO conversion lift: Replaced manual entry pipelines across PAN, Email, Bank, and Liveness checks to eliminate a potential 33.72% drop-off overhead, achieving a +7.8% overall conversion increase.

Key product takeaway

By combining systems engineering (modular Templates & Blocks backend architecture) with contextual, empathetic UI designs, NAO 2.0 removed the typical trade-off between rigorous regulatory compliance (KYC) and a frictionless user experience. Signatures, photos and bank details alone caused 55% of all rejections, and all three could now be caught while the user was still on the screen.

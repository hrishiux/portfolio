Case study 01

Upstox Native Account Opening (NAO) 2.0

Systems-level UX redesign and onboarding architecture overhaul. Upstox is India’s Robinhood equivalent — one of the country’s largest retail broking platforms, with 10M+ registered users and around 3M active traders at the time of this project.

1. Executive summary & strategic business objective

The challenge

In the highly competitive Indian brokerage landscape, user acquisition velocity is directly gated by the onboarding funnel. Upstox’s classic onboarding flow exhibited high drop-off rates due to severe cognitive load, complex regulatory requirements, manual data inputs, and high-latency offline scrutiny.

The baseline performance metrics were:

  • Classic Flow Conversion Rate (Organic): 12.94%
  • Initial Native Account Opening (NAO 1.0) Conversion Rate (Organic): 22.72%
  • Overall Baseline Funnel Conversion (All Channels): 21.47% (with NAO at 31.63%)
  • Rejection Rate: 35.0%
  • Drop-off Rate Across the Funnel: ~60.0%

The objective

The strategic mandate was to redesign and engineer NAO 2.0 to:

  1. Nearly double overall conversion to 60.0%.
  2. Reduce overall step-level rejection rates from 35.0% to 10.0% by replacing post-submission manual audit checks with real-time, automated verification APIs.
  3. Reduce median Time-to-Account Creation from 30 minutes to under 20 minutes, with a downstream system capability to open accounts instantly (under 2 hours).
  4. Decrease customer support overhead (historically, 40.0% of all completed accounts required active sales assistance calls to navigate the funnel).

3. Experience philosophy: the “N-Star” & screen format frameworks

We moved away from isolated, superficial UI updates, framing our redesign around two strategic systems:

A. The “N-Star” experience vision

To align design, engineering, and business operations, we conceptualized a progression framework ranging from a basic functional app to an automated, high-touch platform:

  • 1-Star: User cannot complete signup due to platform bugs.
  • 4-Star: User opens the account in a single session without human assistance.
  • 5-Star: Instant activation. As soon as the user e-signs, they can trade immediately.
  • 6-Star: Zero manual input. Entering a mobile number pulls investment history and net worth in the background with user permission.
  • 10-Star: Founder-level onboarding. Fully personalized experience, direct welcoming calls from executives, premium physical welcome merchandise delivered to the user’s home.

B. The screen format framework

We classified onboarding interfaces into three functional buckets to standardize the UI patterns and cognitive load across all screens:

  1. Simplified Format: Applied to low cognitive-load steps. Focuses on minimizing actions. Example: Email verification via one-tap Google/Apple SSO.
  2. Hand-Holding Format: Applied to high-friction, error-prone steps requiring external lookups. Features in-context instructional overlays, GIFs, or videos. Example: Real-time interactive canvas for digital signatures with explicit boundary-rule cues.
  3. Education Format: Applied when we need the user to retain critical regulatory information or build trust in the platform’s security. Example: Front-loaded platform credibility carousel highlighting Upstox’s 1Cr+ investor base.

4. Platform system architecture overhaul

To support dynamic customization and rapid experimentation, we transitioned from a statically hardcoded step sequence to a modular, backend-driven Templates and Blocks Architecture.

Key technical specs

  • Blocks: Self-contained functional units (e.g. Email Verification Block, Liveness Block, Bank Verification Block) responsible for executing specific inputs, API integrations, and rule processing.
  • Templates: Compiled sequences of connected blocks.
  • Operator Dashboard (CMS): Enables PMs and Engineering Managers to drag, drop, and configure onboarding flows dynamically without code releases.
    • UX Toggles: Toggling explanatory video overlays on/off per step.
    • System Thresholds: Remotely updating backend rule variables (e.g. changing the fuzzy name match pass score from 80 to 90 via CMS).
    • Dynamic Template Assignment: Segmenting users dynamically and serving custom onboarding flows (Templates) based on user demographic or intent.

5. Granular component redesigns (six key blocks)

Block A: Signup, splash, and carousel (the entry point)

The friction point: High latency in cold-app loading, static landing layouts, and manual typing of phone numbers on Android devices.

The redesign solution:

  1. Optimized asset pipeline to establish minimal splash screen time.
  2. Developed a remote-configured, auto-advancing Dynamic Media Carousel Space supporting images, GIFs, and videos. Carousel adjusts dynamically to 3-second intervals for photos, or matches video/GIF playback durations, with auto-adjustment patterns when the virtual keyboard opens.
  3. Integrated the Android Hint Module API to auto-suggest active phone numbers stored on the device, allowing one-tap selection and automating OTP reading and autofill.

Block B: SSO email verification

The friction point: Manual input of email addresses up to 60 characters long resulted in a 7.0% drop-off rate, primarily due to typographic errors, fake emails, or manual OTP entry friction.

The redesign solution:

  1. Implemented native Google Sign-In and Apple Sign-In (exclusive to iOS), converting email verification into a single-tap action.
  2. Engineered a backend verified duplicate-checking routine that validates inputs against existing lead databases and active verified accounts in real time to prevent duplicate signups.
  3. Created a real-time auto-verification mechanism for SSO emails. These accounts bypass manual scrutiny check-queues entirely, dropping off the scrutiny officer’s dashboard to save operational bandwidth.

Block C: Bank account verification

The friction point: Manually typing account numbers, re-entering them, searching for IFSC codes, and uploading cancelled cheques resulted in a 4.53% step-level drop-off and a 15.53% overall rejection rate due to typo mismatch.

The redesign solution:

  1. Introduced a Unified Payment Interface (UPI) Reverse Penny Drop (via Setu): The user initiates a nominal 1 Rupee transaction via whitelisted local UPI apps. Setu captures the account holder’s name, account number, and IFSC directly from the UPI credit-alert webhook, then automatically refunds the 1 Rupee back to the user.
  2. Forward Penny Drop Automation (Backup Path): For manual entries, the system deposits 1 Rupee and triggers real-time checking via Digio API (processing transactions in 4 seconds compared to Razorpay’s 16 seconds).
  3. Implemented a Fuzzy Name Match Algorithm: Matches the fetched bank name with the Income Tax (PAN) record name. A matched score of ≥ 70 allows the user to proceed automatically. Any score below 70 is flagged for manual scrutiny.

Block D: Digital identity & liveness verification

The friction point: Static live photo submission checks took place post-form submission, introducing high processing delays. Poor camera angles or lighting led to an 18.45% photo rejection rate.

The redesign solution:

  1. Integrated HyperVerge Liveness SDK directly into the front-end capture flow, shifting photo verification to a real-time step.
  2. Implemented front-end photo capture guidelines (lighting cues, head centering circle overlay) and dynamic edge-assessment to verify image clarity prior to capture.
  3. Backend performs real-time liveness threshold check (currently configured at an AI confidence score of > 75).
  4. Facial Matching Confidence Score: For non-KRA users, the system compares the live capture against official government records in real time, automatically routing failed matches or low-confidence scores to the scrutiny desk.

Block E: Digital signature canvas

The friction point: Signature rejections accounted for 21.32% of total onboarding rejections, primarily due to signature cropping, illegible scribbles, or users drawing signatures inside a portrait container with restricted width.

The redesign solution:

  1. Designed a GIF/Media-driven priming module showing animated, language-agnostic instructions on how to sign within the boundaries.
  2. Constructed a landscape-oriented digital signature canvas that automatically opens in landscape mode upon interaction, maximizing canvas area and preventing stroke-clipping.
  3. Engineered an absolute zero-state canvas reset policy: If a signature is rejected by scrutiny, the previous canvas state is entirely cleared from the UI and memory to prevent the user from resubmitting an identical failed stroke.
  4. Pixel-Cutoff Regression testing (R&D): Configured real-time pixel-density heuristics to validate signature presence and check for minimum stroke pixel counts before allowing submissions.

Block F: E-Sign custom UI integration

The friction point: The standard NSDL/UIDAI interface was complex, cluttered, and highly drop-off prone (~10.0% overall drop-off on E-Sign), heavily damaging mobile user trust.

The redesign solution:

  1. Collaborated with Setu to replace the standard portal with a customizable mobile-first UI.
  2. Repackaged and surfaced key information clearly to minimize user concern over entering sensitive Aadhaar numbers on non-Upstox interfaces.
  3. Expected step drop-off reduction of ~50.0%.

7. Business impact & metric validation

The rollout of individual modules within the NAO 2.0 initiative delivered immediate, quantifiable improvements across our organic cohorts:

  • pan step — equifax background verification +3.56 pts 71.73% before 75.29% after
  • email step — google / apple sso +4.90 pts 85.00% before 89.90% after

Measured cohort performance

  1. PAN Verification Block (Equifax): Shifting to automated Equifax background verification raised step-level conversion from 71.73% to 75.29% (a +3.56% absolute improvement).
  2. Email Verification Block (SSO): Incorporating Google/Apple SSO elevated conversion from 85.0% to 89.9% (a +4.90% absolute step improvement).
  3. Liveness Verification Block (HyperVerge): Transitioning to real-time image analysis predicted an overall conversion lift of +2.19% by reducing photo drop-offs to 1.0%.
  4. Overall NAO Conversion Lift: Replaced manual entry pipelines across PAN, Email, Bank, and Liveness checks to eliminate a potential 33.72% drop-off overhead, achieving a +7.8% overall conversion increase.

Key product takeaway

By combining systems engineering (modular Templates & Blocks backend architecture) with contextual, empathetic UI designs (such as the landscape digital signature canvas and the Setu customizable E-sign frame), we removed the typical trade-off between rigorous regulatory compliance (KYC) and a frictionless user experience.
